\subsection{Location and Dataset}

In this project, the range of methodologies described previously in this section are focused into a
single pipeline and applied to a real-world dataset, extracting density and abundance estimates
for the chimpanzee population of Taï National Park.

\subsubsection{Taï National Park}

Taï National Park is a protected World Heritage conservation site of great importance, located
in Ivory Coast, West Africa.\cite{whs}
It is one of only few significant rainforested areas remaining in the region, covering an area
of roughly 4500 $\mathrm{km}^\mathrm{2}$.\cite{tnp_deforestation}
It has a rich biodiversity;\cite{biodiversity} however, populations face persistent threat from
predominantly human-induced factors.\cite{wcf_conservation,anti_poaching}, highlighting the urgency for
improved and novel methods of biomonitoring.

\subsubsection{Dataset}\label{subsubsec:dataset}

\textbf{Video and Reference Material}

The dataset, supplied and sourced by the Wild Chimpanzee Foundation,\cite{wcf} contains a large
volume of motion triggered camera trap video capturing chimpanzees from a total of 65 camera trap
point transects.
The camera traps were set up to span a wide area of the park, with clustering in sub-regions.

For each camera trap location, reference videos are supplied that give depth context information
for the camera location.
These videos follow all adopt a consistent referencing method.
Here, a tape measure is placed along the ground in a straight line, beginning at the transect
origin (camera trap) and spanning a distance of fifteen meters.
The reference video is then recorded where a member of the survey team then positions themselves
at one meter distance intervals, facing the camera, along this measured line.
At each distance interval, the team member holds a paper sheet labelled with the corresponding
distance to the camera.

\vspace{5mm}
\textbf{Annotated Data}

Also supplied with the dataset is a large CSV file that holds the annotated data associated with
the camera trap video.
Each row corresponds to a single chimpanzee detection.
For any individual chimpanzee, a row of detection data is annotated at every two second interval
that that is individual is seen in the video.
The first detection corresponds to the moment at which at least half of the chimpanzee's
two-dimensional projected area with respect the camera angle is captured in the camera trap
video frame.
detection data is then record for duration of capture in the frame, with the final detection
following this same principle.

The column structure of the CSV file corresponds to detection metadata used for activity estimation
(i.e., data/time, video/region identifiers, region area, effort) as well as manually estimated
distances for each chimpanzee detection.
Distances are estimated by analysts that use the associated reference material of the camera
trap locations to derive informed approximations.
